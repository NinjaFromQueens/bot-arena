Sense · Think · Act

Every robot does three things. Every lesson has three steps.

01

Learn

A 5-minute lesson with something to try: bounce an ultrasonic sensor off a wall, gear down a motor, bend a robot arm.

02

Build

Pick the parts, set the gear ratio, write the if-then rules. Every choice is a trade-off, just like real engineering.

03

Test

Battle the class, race the clock or fly the mission. Then the game explains why you won or lost, and what to change.

Neo Harbor, 2060.

Fourteen years ago, an AI called OMNI took control of the city “for humanity’s own good.” Now it decides where people live, what they learn and who they talk to.

In the old subway tunnels, a crew of kids is finding what OMNI erased, and learning how its machines really work. Every lesson you finish is one more thing OMNI can’t fool you with.

Questions and answers

What is Circuit Rebels?

Circuit Rebels is a free set of robotics lessons and class games for grades 5 to 8. Students learn how machines sense, think and act in 5-minute lessons, then test what they learned by building robots, racing and flying.

Is it really free?

Yes. Today’s lessons and games are free, with no login. Story mode, teacher tools and class reports are being built, and schools will pay for those later.

Do students need accounts or emails?

No. Students join a class game with a code and a first name or a nickname. The name shows on the class board during the game. It is never stored in a database, sold or shared.

Do we need robot kits?

No. Everything runs in a web browser. Classes that own robot arms or rovers can use them alongside the lessons.

What devices does it run on?

Chromebooks, laptops, tablets and phones. The teacher needs a projector or a board for class games. There is nothing to install.

When does story mode arrive?

Story mode is being built now. Founding Programs in the spring 2027 pilot get first access as it’s ready.
